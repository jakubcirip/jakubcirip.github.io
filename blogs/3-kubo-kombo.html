Motivation and Idea

First of all, I would like to offer you an insight into what motivated me for this project.

Why would someone want a guitar combo?
The answer is simple, they want to play their guitar with a decent sound.
But why not just buy one, but make one? - I like to make simple things more complicated;)
I also like to do something new, create new things, and it's more interesting to me than just buying something.

The basic idea was to use 2 45W speakers that I had left over from the original setup.

Proposal of the inner wall

For better sound properties, my friend, an expert in the field of audio equipment, recommended that I insert an inner wall that will separate the speakers from the electrical equipment and at the same time place damping material on it (on the side near the speakers) so that the sound spreads better outwards from the combo.
This wall will also be useful for placing electrical component (preamps, buffers, amplifier and DC convertor).

JFET Buffer

Impedance Buffer

A buffer is a circuit that isolates one part of a system from another, often acting as a bridge between a high impedance source and a low impedance load.
It ensures signal integrity by preventing loading, distortion, or signal loss, while usually providing current gain.
For the purpose of our combo, it separates the guitar from the preamp module, providing a high input impedance at the input of the preamp module.

Main Characteristic of JFET buffer:

  • It preserved the signal from the guitar without losing quality. It shapes the tone without change.
  • Matches the impedance of the guitar and the preamp input.
  • Amplifies current, not voltage.
  • Separates the guitar from the preamp – the preamp circuit does not affect the guitar pickup.
  • Preserves the dynamics and natural tone of the guitar.

Buffer manufacturing

Buffer components: J201 transistor, 2x capacitor, 3x resistor, PCB board

Buffer Scheme

In this phase, the first step was design of buffer and scheme drawing. The testing program also helped me with simulation of buffer and measurement the values of voltage. For JFET 'follower' buffer is really important to set up the right operating point of JFET transistor.
The DC operating point is the voltage and current in transistor without input signal.
It defines transistor behaviour during signal processing.
If the operating point is set up incorrectly it can cause, that transistor will be 'close' -> means doesn't work, or will be overloaded -> which leads to signal distortion, noise or overheating.
On the first picture we can see the simple buffer with voltage divider for transistor Gate (this voltage help us to set the right operating point). The buffer is without input and output signals and capacitors.

Buffer schematic — without input/output capacitors

On the second picture I added an input source (AC) (which represents the source from the guitar) trought a 1uF input capacitor and an output signal from a transistor Source going trought 4,7uF output capacitor. Using simulation mode we can see the voltage readings on the voltmeters.

Buffer schematic — with AC input and input/output capacitors

Calculation of operating point

Variable definition for operating point:
Vs - Source Voltage
Id - Transformator current

Calculation procedure:

  • 1. Find the DC voltage at the transistor gate:
    $$Vg = {R3 \over R1 + R2 }. Vcc$$
  • 2. We assume that the source stabilizes so that:
    $$Id = {Vs \over Rs }$$

    Values from my measurements: $$Id = {2.09V \over 1000\Omega } = 2.09mA$$

  • 3. Count Vgs
    $$Vgs = {Vg - Vs }$$

    Values from my measurements: $$Vgs = {2.62V - 2.09V = 0.53V }$$

  • 4. Check if this fits with the JFET equation

    Insert values to:

    $$Id = Idss (1 - {Vgs \over Vgs(off) })^2$$

Layout design of controls on the top panel

Problem

To ensure that the amplifier controls—volume, as well as bass, mid, and treble adjustments—remained compact and simple, the layout of these controls had to be carefully considered and designed. This design also had to account for the internal cabling (located beneath the top panel) that connects the various components.
Since the inputs for Channel 1 and Channel 2 are also situated on the top panel, it was necessary to position the respective preamps and volume controls in alignment with their corresponding channels.

Solution

I therefore designed the controls so that the left side serves Channel 1, and the right side serves Channel 2. On the far left is the input for Channel 1, followed to its right by a preamp module with volume, treble, mid, and bass controls for Channel 1. On the far right is the input for Channel 2, with volume, treble, mid, and bass controls for Channel 2 located to its left. The master volume control is positioned exactly in the center.
